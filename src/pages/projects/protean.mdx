Inspired by the [metamon paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04395), protean is a
complete pipeline rather than a single model: raw Showdown battle logs go in one
end, and an agent laddering against humans comes out the other.

## Why the data is the hard part

Pokémon replays are recorded from a spectator's point of view, which sees
everything. An agent has to act from a player's point of view, which does not —
at turn six you do not know the opponent's remaining team, their held items, or
which moves they are running.

Training directly on spectator logs therefore teaches the model to use
information it will never have at inference time. So the pipeline reconstructs
each battle twice, once per player, filling in only what that player could
plausibly have known:

- Unrevealed moves, items and abilities are sampled from Smogon monthly usage
  statistics for the species.
- When fewer than six Pokémon were revealed, the rest of the team is inferred by
  sampling teammate co-occurrence — the same way a human infers "that's the
  standard lead, so they're probably running the standard back four."

The result is 194,715 decision points where the model's view matches what a
player genuinely saw.

## The pipeline

**Parsing** turns raw `.log` text into typed snapshots — active Pokémon, HP,
status, boosts, weather and side conditions per turn — at roughly 1,400 battles
per second.

**Encoding** flattens each snapshot into tensors covering both teams, the field
state and the legal action mask.

**Behaviour cloning** trains a 5.1M-parameter policy to imitate human play,
which gets the agent to competence without ever touching a live server.

**PPO self-play** fine-tunes it against copies of itself on a local Showdown
server, with opponent weights synced to the learner every 50 episodes to keep
the opposition improving alongside the agent. Twenty thousand episodes, with
checkpoints every 500.

**Ladder play** connects the trained policy to Pokémon Showdown over WebSockets
and plays real humans.

## How it actually does

Over 56 rated ladder games the agent went **25–31**, peaking at 1183 and
settling near 1173, against opponents averaging 1168. That is a losing record by
a small margin — and it is the honest number.

What it means in practice is that a policy trained entirely on parsed replays
and self-play holds its own against roughly-equally-rated human ladder players
without ever having been hand-coded with a single piece of Pokémon strategy. It
does not know what a Snorlax is. It learned that from logs.

Self-play showed no catastrophic forgetting — move accuracy against the
behaviour-cloned baseline stayed above 50% throughout fine-tuning.

## What's next

More PPO episodes, a proper evaluation harness against fixed baselines rather
than the live ladder alone, and a search over the reward shaping.
